Restaurant bill format

A restaurant bill is a tax invoice for a service. Here is what it must carry, and a sample you can check your own bill against.

What a restaurant tax invoice shows

  • Restaurant name, address and GSTIN.
  • Bill number (up to 16 characters, one running series per financial year) and date.
  • Each dish or drink with quantity, rate and amount.
  • Discount, if any, before tax.
  • Taxable value, CGST and SGST (or IGST) with their rates, and the total.
  • SAC 9963 (accommodation, food and beverage services) for the restaurant service.
  • The guest’s GSTIN when a company guest gives it, so they can show the expense in their books.

Under composition the bill is a bill of supply without GST, and must say "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies". A restaurant that is not registered gives a plain bill with no GST.

(Dishes and prices are only an example.)

Mistakes to avoid

  • GST at different rates on different dishes. Restaurant service is taxed as one service: the same rate applies to the whole bill.
  • Adding service charge without asking. It is voluntary; see service charge rules.
  • Charging GST when not registered, or under composition. Only a registered restaurant outside composition may collect GST.
  • Alcohol on the GST bill. Alcoholic drinks for human consumption are outside GST and taxed by the state; bill them as your state’s rules require, separately from the GST bill.
